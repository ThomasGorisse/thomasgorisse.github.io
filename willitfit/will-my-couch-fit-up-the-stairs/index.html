Stair width and landing size checks

Will my couch fit up the stairs?

Width and the landing turn are two things you can check, but neither proves a couch will go upstairs. Will It Fit does not simulate stairs. Use the checks below for the narrowest clear stair width and one right-angle landing turn.

Measure the couch’s full width, depth and height. Measure the narrowest usable stair width, including handrails and posts, then the widths before and after the landing and the headroom above it. Check the ceiling over the steps separately.

Before you move it

Can this tell me for sure that my couch will go upstairs?

No. The width check only compares the narrowest object side with the clear stair width. It does not check the height of that carrying position, tilting along the slope, the handrail’s shape or headroom above the steps. Even if both results match, you still need to confirm the whole route and room to change orientation.

How do I check an L-shaped or U-shaped staircase landing?

For one right-angle landing turn, enter the clear approach width before it, the width after it and the lowest headroom above the landing. This is the same level-turn calculation as a hallway. An L-shaped stair normally has one such turn. If a U-shaped stair has two right-angle turns, check each separately with its own measurements; a complete 180-degree turn, finite landing depth and the transition between turns are not simulated.

Where should I measure the stairs?

Measure the narrowest usable width along the route, including handrails, newel posts and wall projections. Measure the clear approaches to the landing and the lowest headroom above it. Separately measure the ceiling over the steps and look for a low beam or sloping ceiling. Allow for hands, padding and room to reposition the couch.

Check the door at the top or bottom of the stairs too.
