Hallway corner checker

Will my couch make the turn in a hallway?

A couch can clear the door and still catch at the corner: its length needs room to swing between the walls. Measure both hallway widths and the ceiling, as well as the couch’s full width, depth and height.

Use the narrowest clear width before and after the turn. Allow for skirting boards, radiators and handles. Standing the couch on its end may help if it clears the ceiling. Leave space for hands and padding.

Before you reach the corner

Why can a couch fit through the door but miss the corner?

A doorway only needs to clear the couch’s cross-section. At a corner, its length must swing between the walls while its inner edge clears the inside corner. That turning space can be the limiting measurement.

Which hallway sizes should I enter?

Measure wall to wall at the narrowest usable points before and after the right-angle turn, and the lowest ceiling above the route. Account for skirting boards, radiators, handles and other obstructions. For a door after the turn, use its clear width and check its height separately.

Can standing the couch on its end help?

Sometimes. This checker tries all six orientations, including ones with the couch’s width pointing up, provided it clears the ceiling. It assumes you can get it into that orientation; lifting it into position or tilting it during the turn is not simulated.
